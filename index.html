What is semantic SEO, and what does the work include?

Semantic SEO optimizes a website for meaning, context and entity relationships instead of keyword repetition. It positions a brand to rank in Google and to earn citations in AI answer engines, such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews.

  • 01

    Entity and topic mapping

    Entity mapping connects the people, places, products and concepts a business is known for. I define the central entity and its attributes, then plan a topical map that covers every question the niche asks, which establishes topical authority.

  • 02

    Content architecture

    Content architecture turns the topical map into clusters of pages joined by internal links. I outline each page around one intent and write answer-first sections and FAQs that large language models (LLMs) can quote directly.

  • 03

    Technical SEO and Core Web Vitals

    Technical SEO keeps a site crawlable, indexable and fast. I audit crawling, indexing and schema markup, and I track Core Web Vitals, such as Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Interaction to Next Paint (INP) and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS), so pages load quickly and stay stable.

  • 04

    AEO and GEO

    Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) prepare a brand for AI-driven discovery. I align entity facts across the site and the wider web, so AI answers describe the business accurately and cite its pages.

Why is semantic SEO important for your business?

Semantic SEO makes your business the clear answer for its topic. Search engines read entities and meaning, so a site that covers its whole topic ranks for more of the questions customers ask, in Google and in AI answers.

  • 01

    Rankings across the whole topic

    A topical map answers each question in your niche, from what a service is to what it costs. Each answer becomes a page or a section, and internal links join them into one topic. According to a 2010 Yahoo! study, over 70% of web queries contained an entity, a type, an attribute or a relation.

  • 02

    A place in AI answers

    Google Search Central states that AI Overviews and AI Mode may run related searches across subtopics to compose one response. A site that covers those subtopics can appear in more of those searches. Clear entity facts, such as names, services and locations, give those answers something precise to quote.

  • 03

    Visitors with buying intent

    Each page matches one search intent, such as comparing prices or booking a service. Visitors land on the page that answers their exact need instead of a general page, so they arrive closer to a call, a booking or a quote request.

  • 04

    A brand Google recognizes

    Consistent facts about your business, such as its name, services, location and profiles, let Google recognize it as an entity. Entity recognition is the first step toward a knowledge panel, and it ties your site, profiles and listings into one trusted record.

Which problems does semantic SEO solve?

Semantic SEO fixes the problems that keep a good business hidden in search. Each card pairs a common problem with the fix I apply.

  • 01

    Pages Google does not index

    New or deep pages stay "Discovered, currently not indexed" when crawlers find no strong path to them. I repair internal links, sitemaps and crawl paths, then request indexing for the pages that matter most.

  • 02

    Pages competing for one search

    Two pages that target one intent split their signals, and neither ranks well. I assign each intent to a single page, then merge or redirect the duplicates into one stronger URL.

  • 03

    Traffic that brings no leads

    Visitors leave when a page answers a different question than the one they searched. I align each page with one buying intent and place the answer, the proof and the next step where readers expect them.

  • 04

    Missing from AI answers

    AI answers pass over brands whose facts conflict across the web. I unify the business name, services and locations across the site, its schema and its profiles, so answer engines can trust and cite them.

  • 05

    Slow or unstable pages

    Heavy images and render-blocking scripts delay Largest Contentful Paint and shift layouts. I audit Core Web Vitals and set fixes with the site's developers, such as compressed images and deferred scripts.

  • 06

    Thin coverage of the topic

    Competitors win the questions a site never answers. I compare the topical map with competing sites and the query network, then plan the missing pages in priority order.

  • Is semantic SEO worth it for a small business?

    Semantic SEO is worth it for most small businesses that win customers through search, because organic visits carry no cost per click once a page ranks. A local service business, such as a plumber or a clinic, can rank for its services, locations and price questions with a focused set of pages.

    Most SEO work, semantic SEO included, needs four months to a year to show results, according to Google's 2017 video on hiring an SEO. Early signs come first, such as new pages getting indexed and long-tail questions starting to rank, and I report them along the way.

    Semantic SEO works best as an ongoing process, although an entity map and a topical map can be delivered as a one-time project. Search results, competitors and AI answers shift over time, so ongoing work keeps your pages accurate, well linked and ready for new queries.

FAQ

What is semantic SEO?

Semantic SEO organizes a website around entities, their attributes and the questions people ask, so search engines understand what each page covers. It establishes topical authority through topical maps, structured content and clear internal links. Search engines such as Google then match the site to whole topics instead of single keywords, which widens the range of queries each page can rank for.

How is semantic SEO different from traditional SEO?

Traditional SEO targets one keyword per page, while semantic SEO targets the entity and its full network of related questions. A traditional page might chase one phrase, such as "plumber near me". A semantic plan covers the services, locations, prices and problems buyers search for and links them, so one site earns topical authority across the whole subject.
