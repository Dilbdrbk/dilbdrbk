How search works, from a query to an AI answer

Five diagrams that explain how I think about search: how a page gets ranked, how AI builds an answer, how an entity becomes a topical map, what a strong page contains, and how I test ideas before trusting them.

01 Chapter

Each stage can fail on its own. Knowing which one is broken decides the fix.

From a search to a ranked page

  1. 01

    The query

    Someone types or speaks a search. The engine first works out the entity, the intent and the kind of result they expect.

  2. 02

    The crawl

    The page is found through links and sitemaps, then fetched. Pages that are hard to reach, slow or blocked may never be read.

  3. 03

    The understanding

    The page is rendered and read. Entities, attributes and their relations are pulled out, and the page is filed under what it really covers.

  4. 04

    The ranking

    Candidate pages are compared on relevance, quality and trust, including how much the whole site knows about the topic.

  5. 05

    The result

    The winner appears as a blue link, a map pack listing, a featured snippet or a source inside an AI answer.

  • Answer in quotable sentences. AI reads passages, not whole pages, so each section should answer its question on its own.
  • Cover the follow-up questions. The smaller searches are the questions your page should already answer.
  • Be consistent everywhere. The same facts about the brand on the site, profiles and communities like Reddit make it a safer source to cite.

Own pageSection on its parent page

  • The source context decides the weight. A repair company and a review site can share an entity but need different pages.
  • Core first, outer second. Money pages form the core section. Supporting guides form the outer section and link up to the core.
  • No page without a job. Pages that split the same intent compete with each other, so they get merged into one.

  1. Title and H1 that match the query

    They name the page's single main topic, in the words people search with.

  2. The answer first

    The opening lines answer the main question directly, before any background.

  3. Entity facts a machine can read

    Attributes such as types, prices, steps and service areas, stated plainly in lists or tables.

  4. Subheadings in the order people ask

    Each H2 answers the next question in the query network.

  5. The follow-up questions

    A short FAQ for what people ask after the main answer.

  6. Internal links with a purpose

    Up to the parent topic, across to siblings, and on to the page that earns.

  7. Structured data

    Schema that names the entity, the business and the type of page.

  8. One clear next step

    A single call to action that fits the intent of the search.

05 Chapter

Much of what I know comes from tests like this, not from rules repeated online.

How I test an SEO idea

  1. 01

    Write the hypothesis

    State the idea as a claim that can fail: this change will lift this metric on these pages.

  2. 02

    Pick test and control

    Choose pages to change and similar pages that stay untouched for comparison.

  3. 03

    Change one variable

    Only one change at a time, so the result can be traced back to it.

  4. 04

    Measure long enough

    Track impressions, clicks and positions until the difference rises above normal movement.

  5. 05

    Decide and record

    Roll it out, refine it or drop it, and write the result down either way.
