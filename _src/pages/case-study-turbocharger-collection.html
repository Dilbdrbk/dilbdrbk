Turbocharger collection page case study: from generic claims to triples and value-based facts

A US diesel parts e-commerce store sells new, rebuilt and used turbochargers through one collection page. I rewrote the page's intro with semantic triples, positive predicates and value ranges, and I added four unique selling points to the hero banner. The page moved from around position 20 to #1 on US Google for 3 turbocharger searches, with citations in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

01 Before

What did the turbocharger page look like before the rewrite?

The turbocharger page opened with generic claims and gave buyers no fact they could check. I reviewed the earlier versions in Wayback Machine captures from February and August 2026.

  • Superlative opening: The intro claimed that nothing matches a brand-new turbocharger for reliability, with no evidence behind it.
  • Unmeasured words: The inventory was "extensive" and the units were "top-rated", with no prices, counts or ranges.
  • A contradiction: The intro said the store sold only new turbochargers, while the product grid below listed rebuilt and used units.
  • No entities: The intro named no turbocharger brands, no engines and no buyer types.
  • An empty hero: The hero banner showed the page name and no reason to buy from this store.

A search engine reading that intro learned only that the store sold turbochargers. A buyer learned nothing about price, fit or stock before scrolling to the products.

02 Triples

How was the intro rewritten with triples and positive predicates?

The new intro is built from semantic triples: a subject, a predicate and an object that a reader and a search engine can parse in one pass. I applied the semantic writing rules of Koray Tugberk Gubur's Topical Authority methodology, which I learned and use on client pages.

SubjectPredicateObject
A replacement turbochargerrestorescharge pressure, power and fuel economy
A replacement turbochargerremovesfailure signs, such as black smoke and slow acceleration
The storesellsnew, rebuilt and used turbochargers
The turbochargersfitdiesel engines from makers such as Cummins and Volvo

The table shows the pattern in my own wording, not the client's copy. Positive predicates, such as "restores" and "removes", describe what the product does for the buyer. In my work, this framing keeps an intro on the outcome the buyer searched for: an engine back at full power.

03 Values

Which average and range values went into the intro?

The new intro replaces unmeasured words with values taken from the store's own listings. Every value is something a buyer can check against the product grid.

Value in the introWhat it tells the buyer
Price range, from the lowest to the highest listingThe budget the collection covers
Share of listings under $1,000How much of the range fits a common repair budget
Engine size range in litersWhich engines the units fit, from pickups to industrial machines
Share of listings with cross-reference or OE numbersHow easily a buyer can match an old part number
Count of CHRA cartridgesThat rebuild parts sit next to complete units
Free shipping thresholdThe order value that removes the shipping cost

The exact figures stay off this page to keep the client anonymous. On the live page, each one is a specific number or range.

04 Entities

Which entities and benefits did the intro add?

The intro now connects the turbocharger to the entities a buyer compares. It names the buyers, the brands, the engines and the product itself.

  • Buyers: Remanufacturers, parts brokers and fleets
  • Brands: Turbocharger makers, such as Garrett, BorgWarner, Holset, Mitsubishi and IHI
  • Engines: Diesel engine makers, such as Cummins, Volvo, Caterpillar, John Deere and Detroit
  • Definition: A turbocharger is an exhaust-driven unit in which a turbine spins a compressor that pushes denser air into the engine.

Below the intro, 9 benefits each follow a cause and effect, such as a working replacement turbo restoring the charge pressure a failed unit lost. Each benefit names one engine outcome, so it answers one buyer question.

06 Results

What results did the rewrite produce?

The collection page moved from around position 20, on Google's second page, onto the first page for 6 turbocharger searches, with 3 of them at #1. I checked every result live on 18 November 2026 in US Google results with personalization turned off.

The starting point of around position 20 comes from my own rank checks before the rewrite, so it is approximate. The screenshots come from the same live checks, with every client detail blurred.

The page does not reach the first page for broad head terms, such as "turbochargers for sale", where performance turbo brands hold most positions. The searches it wins combine the three conditions the intro names: new, rebuilt and used.

07 Lessons

What does this case study show about collection page SEO?

The strongest results came on searches that match the intro's own triples, such as new, rebuilt and used turbochargers. In my work, an intro that states what the store sells, who buys it and which values apply gives search engines and AI answers a clear passage to quote.

Rankings in this table come from live checks, not a tracking export, so positions can shift from day to day. Other examples will appear on the case studies page as each one is measured.
